# Surgical Antimicrobial Prophylaxis Protocol

> **SYNTHETIC TEST DOCUMENT — NOT CLINICAL GUIDANCE.** Written to exercise the
> ingest, indexing and retrieval pipeline. The recommendations below are
> plausible in shape but must never be used for patient care.

## Scope

This protocol covers antimicrobial prophylaxis for adults undergoing elective and
emergency surgery in theatres. It does not cover treatment of established
infection, for which the antimicrobial stewardship policy applies.

## Timing

The single most important determinant of efficacy is timing. Administer the
prophylactic dose within 60 minutes before knife-to-skin, so that tissue
concentrations are at their peak when the incision is made.

Vancomycin and fluoroquinolones require a 120-minute window because they must be
infused slowly. Start these at induction or earlier, and never rush the infusion
to meet the incision time.

A dose given after the incision provides no benefit and should be documented as
a protocol deviation.

## Single Dose Principle

Prophylaxis means one dose. Prolonging prophylaxis beyond wound closure does not
reduce surgical site infection and does increase Clostridioides difficile
infection, resistance and cost.

### Intraoperative Redosing

Redose intraoperatively when the procedure exceeds two half-lives of the agent,
or when blood loss exceeds 1500 mL. For cefazolin this means redosing at 4 hours;
for cefuroxime at 4 hours; for metronidazole no redose is required.

Redosing is not an extension of prophylaxis — it maintains the concentration of
the single perioperative exposure.

### Drains and Catheters

Do not continue prophylaxis because a drain, catheter or line remains in place.
There is no evidence that it prevents device-related infection, and the practice
is a common source of unnecessary antibiotic days.

## Agent Selection by Procedure

### Clean Procedures

For clean orthopaedic, vascular and cardiac surgery, cefazolin 2 g intravenously
is the standard agent, increased to 3 g for patients weighing 120 kg or more.

Prophylaxis is not indicated for clean procedures without prosthetic material,
such as simple hernia repair without mesh or excision of a skin lesion.

### Clean-Contaminated Procedures

Colorectal surgery requires cover for Gram-negative and anaerobic organisms:
cefuroxime 1.5 g plus metronidazole 500 mg intravenously. Oral neomycin and
erythromycin with mechanical bowel preparation may be added where local policy
supports it.

Biliary, gastroduodenal and gynaecological surgery are covered by cefuroxime 1.5
g alone.

### Penicillin Allergy

Establish the nature of the reported reaction. A rash without features of
anaphylaxis is not a contraindication to a cephalosporin: the cross-reactivity
rate is under 2%, and substituting vancomycin unnecessarily is associated with
higher surgical site infection rates.

For genuine severe allergy, use teicoplanin plus gentamicin for clean procedures,
adding metronidazole where anaerobic cover is required.

## Special Populations

### Renal Impairment

Prophylactic doses are single doses and generally need no adjustment for renal
function. Gentamicin is the exception: use a single 3 mg/kg dose and avoid it
altogether where the estimated glomerular filtration rate is below 20 mL/min.

### Elderly and Frail Patients

Weight-based dosing matters more in this group because lean body mass is
frequently overestimated. Review concurrent nephrotoxins before adding
gentamicin, and prefer agents that do not require monitoring.

## MRSA Colonisation

Patients known to be colonised with meticillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus
receive teicoplanin in addition to the standard agent for their procedure.
Decolonisation with nasal mupirocin and chlorhexidine body wash should begin 5
days before elective surgery.

## Audit and Compliance

Theatre teams record the agent, dose and administration time on the WHO checklist
at the sign-in step. Compliance with the 60-minute window is audited monthly, and
results are reported to the surgical governance meeting.
