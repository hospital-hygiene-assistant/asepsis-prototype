# Type 2 Diabetes Management

## Diagnosis

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is diagnosed when any of the following criteria are confirmed on two occasions (or once with unequivocal hyperglycaemia symptoms): fasting plasma glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L (126 mg/dL); 2-hour glucose ≥11.1 mmol/L on 75 g OGTT; HbA1c ≥48 mmol/mol (6.5%); or random glucose ≥11.1 mmol/L with classic symptoms.

Prediabetes is defined by fasting glucose 5.6–6.9 mmol/L, 2-hour OGTT glucose 7.8–11.0 mmol/L, or HbA1c 39–47 mmol/mol. Intensive lifestyle intervention reduces progression to T2DM by 58% (Diabetes Prevention Program).

## Glycaemic Targets

HbA1c target for most adults with T2DM is <53 mmol/mol (7.0%). A tighter target of <48 mmol/mol (6.5%) may be pursued in younger patients with short disease duration, no cardiovascular disease, low hypoglycaemia risk, and good life expectancy. A relaxed target of <64 mmol/mol (8.0%) is appropriate for elderly or frail patients, those with limited life expectancy, and patients with recurrent severe hypoglycaemia or impaired hypoglycaemia awareness.

Fasting glucose target: 4.0–7.0 mmol/L. Two-hour postprandial glucose target: <8.5 mmol/L. In patients using continuous glucose monitoring (CGM), time-in-range 70–180 mg/dL >70%, time below range <4%, and time above range <25% are complementary targets. Sensor-augmented insulin delivery systems should be used where available for type 1 diabetes; their role in T2DM is expanding.

## Pharmacological Management

### Metformin

Metformin remains the preferred initial glucose-lowering agent for most patients with T2DM unless contraindicated. It reduces HbA1c by approximately 1.0–1.5%, is weight neutral or modestly weight-reducing, has a very low risk of hypoglycaemia, and is inexpensive. It acts primarily by reducing hepatic glucose output and improving insulin sensitivity.

Initiation: 500 mg once or twice daily with food, titrated over 4–8 weeks to 1000 mg twice daily (maximum 2500–3000 mg/day). The most common side effects are gastrointestinal (nausea, diarrhoea, abdominal discomfort) and are minimised by dose titration and administration with meals; modified-release formulations reduce GI side effects. Metformin is contraindicated when eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m² (accumulation risk for lactic acidosis) and should be temporarily withheld before contrast procedures and major surgery. Vitamin B12 deficiency can occur with long-term use; annual measurement is recommended.

### Second-Line Agent Selection

#### Established Cardiovascular Disease

In patients with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD — prior MI, stroke, or symptomatic peripheral arterial disease), GLP-1 receptor agonists with proven cardiovascular benefit are the preferred second agent after metformin: semaglutide (0.5–1 mg weekly SC; SUSTAIN-6 and PIONEER 6), liraglutide (1.2–1.8 mg daily SC; LEADER trial), and dulaglutide (1.5 mg weekly SC; REWIND trial) all demonstrated significant reductions in major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in dedicated outcome trials.

SGLT-2 inhibitors also have ASCVD indication: empagliflozin (EMPA-REG OUTCOME) and canagliflozin (CANVAS) showed MACE reduction. SGLT-2 inhibitors additionally reduce hospitalisation for heart failure and slow CKD progression, making them valuable in patients with multiple comorbidities. When cost is a consideration and neither GLP-1 agonist nor SGLT-2 inhibitor is accessible, pioglitazone (evidence from PROactive trial for recurrent stroke and MI reduction) or sulfonylureas may be used.

#### Heart Failure and CKD

SGLT-2 inhibitors are the agents of choice in patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) and/or CKD regardless of HbA1c level, primarily for their cardiorenal protective effects rather than glucose lowering. Dapagliflozin (DAPA-HF, DAPA-CKD), empagliflozin (EMPEROR-Reduced, EMPA-KIDNEY), and canagliflozin (CREDENCE) demonstrated significant reductions in heart failure hospitalisation, cardiovascular death, and kidney disease progression in patients with and without diabetes.

In patients with eGFR 20–45 mL/min/1.73 m², SGLT-2 inhibitors remain effective for cardiorenal protection (dose-cap applies for glucose lowering). Below eGFR 20, SGLT-2 inhibitors should not be used for glucose lowering. DPP-4 inhibitors (sitagliptin, alogliptin) are safe in heart failure and CKD with dose adjustment for eGFR, though saxagliptin and alogliptin demonstrated modest increases in heart failure hospitalisation and should be avoided. Metformin dose should be reduced or stopped as eGFR declines below 45.

#### Weight and Hypoglycaemia

When weight reduction is the priority, GLP-1 receptor agonists are the most effective glucose-lowering agents: semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly (STEP trials) achieves 12–15% body weight reduction on average; liraglutide 3 mg daily achieves 5–8%. Tirzepatide (dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist, SURPASS and SURMOUNT trials) provides 15–22% weight reduction and has recently received approval for obesity.

When minimising hypoglycaemia risk is the priority (impaired awareness, elderly patients, occupation requiring alertness), SGLT-2 inhibitors, DPP-4 inhibitors, and GLP-1 agonists are preferred over sulfonylureas or insulin. If a sulfonylurea is required for cost reasons, gliclazide modified release has the lowest hypoglycaemia risk among this class. When using insulin, basal analogues (glargine U-100/U-300, degludec) have a more predictable pharmacokinetic profile and lower nocturnal hypoglycaemia risk than NPH insulin.

### Insulin Therapy

Insulin is indicated when HbA1c remains significantly elevated despite maximal tolerated oral therapy, when presentation HbA1c is >86 mmol/mol (10%), or when symptomatic hyperglycaemia is present. Basal insulin (insulin glargine 100 U/mL or 300 U/mL, insulin detemir, or insulin degludec) is initiated at 10 units/day or 0.1–0.2 U/kg/day and titrated based on fasting glucose (target 4.0–7.0 mmol/L) by 2 units every 3 days.

If fasting glucose target is achieved but HbA1c remains above goal, postprandial glucose is the driver: add rapid-acting insulin analogue before the main meal (step-up approach), or switch to pre-mixed insulin formulations. Insulin degludec/insulin aspart (IDegAsp) and insulin glargine/lixisenatide (iGlarLixi) fixed-ratio combinations simplify regimens and reduce injection frequency. Self-monitoring of blood glucose (4–7 point profiles for insulin initiation and adjustment) and structured education in sick-day rules are essential.

## Complication Screening

Annual screening is recommended for all patients with T2DM to detect microvascular and macrovascular complications at the earliest reversible stage. Diabetic retinopathy screening: dilated fundoscopy or 45° digital retinal photography (mydriatic preferred for sensitivity). Frequency: annually until two consecutive normal screens, then every two years if controlled. Findings of any retinopathy should prompt ophthalmology review; proliferative retinopathy or macular oedema requires urgent laser photocoagulation or intravitreal anti-VEGF therapy.

Diabetic nephropathy screening: urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR) on a spot morning sample and serum creatinine/eGFR annually. ACR 3–30 mg/mmol (microalbuminuria) warrants RAAS inhibition to slow progression. ACR >30 mg/mmol (macroalbuminuria/overt nephropathy) indicates established nephropathy; RAAS inhibition, SGLT-2 inhibitor, and BP target <130/80 mmHg are all indicated. eGFR decline should be monitored: a fall >5 mL/min/1.73 m² per year warrants nephrology referral.

Diabetic neuropathy screening: 10 g monofilament test (loss of protective sensation), 128 Hz tuning fork (vibration threshold), and ankle reflex assessment annually. Ipswich touch test is a simple validated alternative. Peripheral arterial disease assessment: ABI measurement in patients with symptoms or foot abnormalities. Foot inspection at every clinic visit for ulcers, calluses, nail changes, or deformity.

Cardiovascular risk assessment: QRISK3 (UK) or PCE (US) calculator at diagnosis and annually. Statin therapy (atorvastatin 20 mg minimum, 40–80 mg for high-risk patients) is recommended for all patients aged ≥40 with T2DM or younger patients with additional risk factors or organ damage. LDL target: <2.0 mmol/L (or <1.4 mmol/L for very high-risk patients, European guidelines). ACE inhibitor or ARB is recommended in all patients with albuminuria; aspirin for primary prevention is no longer routinely recommended.

Structured diabetes education (DESMOND for T2DM, X-PERT programme) should be offered at diagnosis. Annual HbA1c, lipid profile, blood pressure, BMI, and renal and liver function tests (for metformin and pioglitazone monitoring) form the core annual review.
