# Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy

> **SYNTHETIC TEST DOCUMENT — NOT CLINICAL GUIDANCE.** Written to exercise the
> ingest, indexing and retrieval pipeline. The recommendations below are
> plausible in shape but must never be used for patient care.

## Classification

Chronic hypertension is hypertension present before 20 weeks of gestation or
before conception. Gestational hypertension arises after 20 weeks without
proteinuria or organ dysfunction. Pre-eclampsia is new hypertension after 20
weeks with proteinuria or maternal organ dysfunction.

Severe hypertension in pregnancy is a systolic pressure of 160 mmHg or above, or
a diastolic pressure of 110 mmHg or above, confirmed on repeat measurement
within 15 minutes.

## Diagnosis and Assessment

### Blood Pressure Measurement

Measure with the woman seated and the arm supported at heart level, using a cuff
sized to the mid-arm circumference. Korotkoff phase 5 defines the diastolic
pressure. Automated devices under-read in pre-eclampsia and should be validated
against a manual reading at least once per admission.

### Investigating Suspected Pre-eclampsia

Send full blood count, creatinine, liver transaminases, and a urine
protein:creatinine ratio. A ratio of 30 mg/mmol or above is significant. Placental
growth factor testing between 20 and 35 weeks helps rule out pre-eclampsia where
the diagnosis is uncertain.

Fetal assessment comprises ultrasound biometry, amniotic fluid volume and
umbilical artery Doppler at diagnosis, repeated every 2 weeks while expectant
management continues.

## Antihypertensive Treatment

### Agent Selection

Labetalol is the first-line oral agent. Nifedipine modified-release is the
alternative where labetalol is contraindicated, most often in asthma. Methyldopa
is a third-line option and should be stopped within 2 days of birth because of
its association with postnatal depression.

ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers and direct renin inhibitors are
contraindicated throughout pregnancy: they are fetotoxic, causing oligohydramnios,
renal failure and skull hypoplasia. Women of childbearing age taking these agents
must be switched before conception wherever pregnancy is planned.

Diuretics are avoided because plasma volume is already reduced in pre-eclampsia.

### Treatment Targets

Treat to a target of 135/85 mmHg or below. Do not lower the pressure abruptly:
placental perfusion depends on maternal pressure, and a rapid fall precipitates
fetal distress.

### Acute Severe Hypertension

Severe hypertension requires treatment within 30 minutes. Options are oral
nifedipine, intravenous labetalol, or intravenous hydralazine. Continuous fetal
monitoring is required during and for 30 minutes after intravenous treatment.

## Seizure Prophylaxis and Treatment

Magnesium sulphate is given for eclamptic seizures and for prophylaxis in severe
pre-eclampsia. The loading dose is 4 g intravenously over 5–15 minutes, followed
by an infusion of 1 g per hour for 24 hours.

Monitor deep tendon reflexes, respiratory rate and urine output hourly. Loss of
reflexes is the earliest sign of toxicity. Calcium gluconate 1 g intravenously is
the antidote and must be immediately available at the bedside.

Reduce the infusion rate in renal impairment: magnesium is renally cleared, and
oliguria causes accumulation to toxic concentrations within hours.

## Timing of Birth

Birth before 34 weeks is reserved for uncontrollable hypertension, eclampsia,
pulmonary oedema, placental abruption or non-reassuring fetal status. Give
antenatal corticosteroids and magnesium for neuroprotection where preterm birth
is anticipated.

Between 34 and 37 weeks, continue expectant management with close surveillance
unless maternal or fetal indications supervene. At 37 weeks or beyond, offer
birth within 24–48 hours of diagnosis.

## Postnatal Care

Blood pressure typically peaks 3–6 days after birth, often after discharge. Every
woman treated for hypertension in pregnancy needs a documented blood pressure
review within 7 days.

Enalapril is compatible with breastfeeding and is a suitable postnatal switch for
women who need ongoing treatment. Avoid diuretics while breastfeeding.

Women who had pre-eclampsia carry a lifetime cardiovascular risk roughly double
that of the background population, and should be offered annual blood pressure
and metabolic review.
