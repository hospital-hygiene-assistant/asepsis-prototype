# Drug Dosing in Renal Impairment

> **SYNTHETIC TEST DOCUMENT — NOT CLINICAL GUIDANCE.** Written to exercise the
> ingest, indexing and retrieval pipeline. The recommendations below are
> plausible in shape but must never be used for patient care.

## Assessing Renal Function

### Which Estimate to Use

Use the Cockcroft–Gault estimate of creatinine clearance for drug dosing, not the
eGFR reported automatically by the laboratory. Reported eGFR is normalised to a
body surface area of 1.73 m² and misleads at the extremes of body size — it
overestimates clearance in a small elderly woman and underestimates it in a large
young man.

### When Creatinine Misleads

Serum creatinine lags behind a falling glomerular filtration rate by 24–48 hours.
In acute kidney injury, dose on the trajectory rather than the current value: a
creatinine that has doubled in a day represents a far lower clearance than the
absolute number suggests.

Creatinine also underestimates impairment in patients with low muscle mass —
the elderly, the malnourished, amputees, and those with cirrhosis.

## Antimicrobials

### Aminoglycosides

Gentamicin is dosed at 5–7 mg/kg using ideal body weight, with the interval
extended rather than the dose reduced. Extending the interval preserves the peak
concentration that drives bacterial killing while allowing a trough low enough to
limit accumulation.

Take a trough level before the second dose where clearance is below 30 mL/min.
Stop if the trough exceeds 1 mg/L, and do not simply give the next dose on time.

Monitor for vestibular toxicity daily. It is frequently irreversible and is
detected late if the only monitoring is biochemical.

### Beta-Lactams

Most penicillins and cephalosporins require dose reduction below a clearance of
30 mL/min. Reduce the frequency rather than the individual dose, since efficacy
depends on the time the concentration stays above the minimum inhibitory
concentration.

Neurotoxicity — myoclonus, confusion, seizures — is the characteristic sign of
beta-lactam accumulation and is regularly misattributed to sepsis or delirium in
an unwell patient.

### Vancomycin

Dose by area under the curve rather than by trough where the service supports it.
Where troughs are used, target 15–20 mg/L for deep-seated infection.

In dialysis, dose after the session, not before, or a substantial fraction of the
dose is removed by the filter.

## Cardiovascular Agents

Renally cleared beta-blockers such as atenolol and sotalol accumulate; bisoprolol
and metoprolol are hepatically cleared and are safer choices in impairment.

Digoxin requires both a lower loading and a lower maintenance dose. Its toxic
threshold falls further with hypokalaemia, so the dose cannot be considered in
isolation from the potassium.

ACE inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers reduce glomerular filtration
pressure by design. A creatinine rise of up to 30% after initiation is expected
and is not a reason to stop; a rise beyond that warrants review for renal artery
stenosis or volume depletion.

## Analgesia

Avoid non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs entirely below a clearance of 30
mL/min. They are the single most common avoidable cause of drug-induced acute
kidney injury in inpatients.

Morphine accumulates as the active metabolite morphine-6-glucuronide, causing
delayed sedation and respiratory depression up to 24 hours after the last dose.
Oxycodone and fentanyl are preferred. Gabapentin and pregabalin need substantial
reduction and are a frequent cause of unexplained drowsiness on the ward.

## Monitoring After a Dose Change

Recheck renal function within 48 hours of any dose adjustment, and again after
any new nephrotoxin, contrast exposure or episode of hypotension.

Document the clearance used and the date it was calculated on the prescription
itself, so that the next prescriber can see what the dose was based on rather
than recalculating from a stale value.
