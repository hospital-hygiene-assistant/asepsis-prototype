# Maintenance Notes for a Small Sailing Dinghy

## Hull and Fittings

Rinse the hull with fresh water after every outing and inspect the centreboard slot for grit. Tighten the gunwale bolts at the start of each season; a loose gunwale is the usual source of the creak heard when tacking. Gelcoat chips below the waterline should be filled within a week to stop water wicking into the laminate.

## Rigging

Halyards stretch over their first season and should be re-marked at the cleat once the mast has settled. Replace the main halyard when the outer braid shows more than three broken strands within any 20 cm length. Shrouds are tensioned so that the leeward shroud goes just slack on a close-hauled course in a moderate breeze.

## Sails

Fold the mainsail along the same creases each time and store it dry. Ultraviolet damage shows first along the leech; a leech that has gone chalky and stiff will soon tear along the batten pockets. Wash the jib in fresh water twice a season and never with detergent.

## Trailer and Launching

Grease the winch and the hub bearings twice a year, and more often if the trailer is dipped in salt water. Check tyre pressures before every tow; an under-inflated trailer tyre overheats within twenty miles. Launch bow-first on a shallow ramp and keep the hubs out of the water where possible.

## Winter Storage

Store the boat upside down on padded trestles with the drain bungs removed. Loosen every shroud and halyard by a few turns so the mast is not under constant load, and cover the whole boat with a breathable cover rather than a plastic sheet, which traps condensation.
